## 行く（いく）

### Nuance

**Before**

```text
行く（いく） describes going to a destination. 日曜日は図書館に行きます describes a regular Sunday visit to the library; に marks the destination.
```

**After**

```text
行く（いく） describes going to another place, generally viewed as movement away from the speaker's or current reference point. It can also describe visiting or attending a place. The polite nonpast 行きます can describe habits or future plans, depending on context.
```

### Logic

**Before**

```text
The person moves toward the named destination, 図書館. The sentence uses the polite nonpast, which can describe this habit.
```

**After**

```text
行く describes movement to a destination. 図書館に identifies that destination. The nonpast form alone does not determine whether the visit is habitual or planned.
```

## 細かい（こまかい）

### Nuance

**Before**

```text
細かい（こまかい） describes small or fine details. In this sentence it means she is fussy about minor details, a mildly critical description.
```

**After**

```text
細かい describes small or fine parts, detailed information, or close attention to minor distinctions. It can describe careful precision positively or excessive attention to details negatively, depending on context. In 細かいことにうるさい, the criticism is expressed by the complete phrase.
```

### Logic

**Before**

```text
The sentence focuses on her attention to small particulars; here that attention becomes fussiness. 細 is only a learning cue for fineness, not an etymological explanation.
```

**After**

```text
細かい centers on small, fine-grained parts or distinctions. Applied to こと, it points to minor details; with うるさい, it describes being fussy about them.
```

### Meaning

**Before**

```text
fussy about details; particular
```

**After**

```text
fine; small; detailed; meticulous; sometimes nitpicky, depending on what is described
```

### Difference

**Before**

```text
細かいことにうるさい means fussy about details; 細かな means detailed/fine and uses the na-adjective form.
```

**After**

```text
細かい describes fineness or attention to detail. 細かいことにうるさい specifically describes being fussy about minor details. The negative judgment comes from the complete expression.
```

## 姪（めい）

### Nuance

**Before**

```text
姪（めい） means niece, a sibling’s daughter. The Japanese is singular: “my niece is three years old”; the deck English mistakenly says “nieces.”
```

**After**

```text
姪（めい） means a sibling’s daughter. Japanese does not mark number here, so the sentence may refer to one niece or more; the English deck translation uses “nieces.”
```

### Logic

**Before**

```text
私の姪は3才です identifies one niece and states her age.
```

**After**

```text
姪 identifies a kinship relationship: the daughter of one's sibling. 私の establishes the relationship to the speaker. The noun itself does not specify whether one niece or several nieces are meant.
```

## 交渉（こうしょう）

### Nuance

**Before**

```text
交渉 means negotiation. The sentence says the price is currently being negotiated; Japanese leaves the subject unspecified.
```

**After**

交渉 is discussion between parties to arrange terms or resolve an issue. It names the negotiating process, not a guaranteed agreement; negotiations may continue, succeed, or break down.

## 歌（うた）

### Nuance

**Before**

```text
歌 means a song. In this sentence, the speaker says they did not know that particular song.
```

**After**

```text
歌（うた） usually means a song or singing. In literary contexts, it can also refer to Japanese poetry, especially waka. Here その歌 refers to a particular song.
```

### Logic

**Before**

```text
The word refers to a piece of music with a melody and usually words; the sentence marks it as already identifiable with その.
```

**After**

```text
歌 centers on verbal artistic expression through singing or poetic form. Depending on context, it can refer to a song, singing, or traditional poetry.
```

### Meaning

**Before**

```text
song
```

**After**

```text
song; singing; poem in literary contexts
```

## パチンコ（パチンコ）

### Nuance

**Before**

```text
パチンコ is the Japanese pinball-like machine game. しています describes playing it, and 毎日 says this happens every day.
```

**After**

```text
パチンコ is a Japanese machine game in which small steel balls are launched through pins toward winning pockets. パチンコをする is the ordinary expression for playing it.
```

## 留学（りゅうがく）

### Nuance

**Before**

```text
留学 means studying abroad. The sentence says the speaker studied in the United States for a year; the Japanese omits the subject that the English supplies as “I.”
```

**After**

```text
留学 means going to another place, commonly another country, to study for a period of time. Study is the purpose of the stay, unlike travel alone; the destination and duration come from context.
```
